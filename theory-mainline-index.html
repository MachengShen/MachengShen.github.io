0. How to read this page

standing marks a stable signpost in the current framework; working marks an idea worth developing but still answerable to formalization or evidence; speculative marks an exploratory bridge or application that may be downgraded or dropped. These are epistemic states, not grades of truth.

You do not need to agree with the page from top to bottom. Pick two or three hooks: one that feels powerful, one that feels suspect, and perhaps one that touches your own field. Starting from a counterexample usually makes for the better conversation.

Glossary: six useful terms

it-from-bitA direction of inquiry: perhaps what we call physical “things” become definite through distinguishable, recordable informational relations. It is not shorthand for “the universe is a computer.”
Decoherent historiesAfter a quantum system interacts with its environment, some alternative histories no longer show observable interference and can be discussed like non-conflicting classical stories. This helps explain classical appearance; it does not automatically settle every measurement question.
Reachability setThe states that can actually be reached from a present state under given laws and resources. If information has physical force, it should change this set rather than merely redescribe it.
Coarse-graining / RGCoarse-graining ignores detail to retain effective large-scale variables; the renormalization group (RG) studies what survives as the scale of description changes. The question here is whether stable laws emerge from disciplined information loss.
Morphism / category priorityAsk first which transformations between things are allowed and how those transformations compose, then ask what the things “are.” This is a priority for explanation, not a claim that objects do not exist.
Encoding ≠ ontologyOne system may faithfully encode part of another system's structure without becoming the same kind of being. A model computing the right result does not by itself tell us what reality is made of.

2. Mainline index

Status: standing locked · working in progress · speculative speculative

2.1 Mainline = information ontology / cosmic substrate

The core claim is not merely that information is useful; it is that a basic account of physical reality may need to begin with distinctions, relations, and lawful transformations. It does not say that everything is text, numbers, or bits inside a computer. A friend can push back by asking what this language explains beyond ordinary physics and what observation could make it wrong.

umbrella · standing
2.2 Records / correlations make reality definite; universe-as-distributed-memory is a useful intuition

This connects “definitely happened” with stable correlations left in the world: traces can be distributed through an environment without waiting for a human observer. It does not give the universe human memory or intention; “distributed memory” is an intuition for thinking about records. The pressure test is to ask which physical conditions make a correlation a record, and why fleeting entanglement is not enough.

T1 · working · intuition ≠ final law
2.3 Information Dynamics: lawful update; information changes future reachability

This treats dynamics as rule-constrained update and asks whether possessing information changes what a system can do next. It does not make information a magical force detached from energy, carriers, and constraints. The cleanest objection is to demand two physically distinguishable reachability sets, not merely two descriptions; the public ledger gives the plain-language definition.

T2 · working · second layer of the mainline; formalization still being tightened
2.4 The Reachable World (14 pieces) = a continuous reachability explainer

This continuous popular explainer connects what a state is, which paths constraints close, and which futures remain open. Its chapters are not yet individually online, but the public ledger now gives the arc and common reachability definition. It is not a completed peer-reviewed theory; readers can still test whether the eventual fourteen pieces share an operational meaning or only an attractive metaphor.

T2 · working · continuous explainer; no invented links for chapters not yet online
2.5 Euler's identity as a public hook for “one generative mechanism, many projections”

Euler's identity is a vivid way to show how seemingly different representations can arise from one structure and meet under special conditions. It does not prove information ontology, and mathematical beauty is not physical evidence. Push back by asking whether there is a specified structure-preserving map or only an elegant analogy.

T4-adjacent · working · public page (exploratory; not a final physics verdict)
2.6 Mainline targeting sieve: correlation/records, scale & coarse-graining, relational structure, quantum information dynamics, mapping discipline

These themes are filters for load-bearing work: they touch records, scale, relations, dynamics, and testable mappings. They are not a keyword net that accepts anything mentioning “quantum” or “information.” For each candidate, ask which thread it joins, what it explains, and where its mapping breaks.

T1–T4 · standing · branch mismatch ≠ automatic HOLD
2.7 Four-layer information test + Marr guardrails

The same word “information” may refer to a physical carrier, an algorithm or representation, a functional goal, or an observer-level interpretation; the four-layer test keeps those meanings apart. The Marr guardrail separates the problem being solved, the representation and computation, and the implementing mechanism. Stress-test any popular video by asking whether it silently jumps between these layers.

epistemology · working · red-pill-style content = trigger, not authority
2.8 Public Spine page = multi-agent collaboration spine

Spine displays an engineering skeleton for giving multiple actors shared structure, boundaries, and hand-off points. It may teach us how relations and updates can be organized, but it cannot prove that nature uses the same ontology. Treat it as a counterexample factory: which conveniences are artifacts of human design and cannot be projected onto the cosmos?

engineering branch · working · spine/ · engineering evidence, not ontology evidence
2.9 Endogenous objectives + multi-agent orchestration = branch

This branch studies how goals can form within a system and how several actors can coordinate without losing boundaries or feedback. It is not evidence for information ontology and commits the mainline to no particular agent architecture. If these engineering questions can be fully solved without ontology language, the branch should remain demoted.

engineering branch · standing · ask first whether the mainline is touched
2.10 Body-net / Dream-RSI / discovery-tree = applied forks

These labels name proposals that carry records, updates, feedback, or search structures into bodily sensing, self-improvement, and discovery workflows. They are not established laws of nature, and product performance cannot prove an ontology backward. For each fork, ask which mainline assumption is indispensable and whether failure would really count against the mainline.

applied fork · speculative · cannot replace the mainline
2.11 Constructor theory (Deutsch) bridge

Constructor theory organizes physical explanation around which transformations are possible and impossible, so it may connect naturally to reachability language. For now this is a known gap, not a completed bridge, and it asserts no institutional or intellectual affiliation between people or projects. Progress would require a term-by-term mapping, one successful case, and one explicit breaking point.

speculative · known gap (not fabricated); build separately if needed
2.12 How a consciousness interface arises: Consciousness Dynamics public digest

The working sketch treats mind-like modes as attractors (threat, deficit-ingest, autopilot, competitive, glow-preserve, a learnable “human” mode, and so on), and defines W coarsely as awareness of which mode-gate is firing. At low W the deepest available autopilot wins; the minimal self-maintenance claim is to see the gate before the old map runs. Six-realm language is marked metaphor; equations / RealmGate / W are design hypotheses — not an ontology of afterlife places, and not a clinical tool. Public entry: consciousness-dynamics-v0.1-public.md. Light neighbors: hub is a blackboard, not a mind; coupling lexicon (zh).

consciousness structure · speculative · design_hypothesis + metaphor · non-clinical
2.13 How a self is maintained: continuity as attractor re-instantiation, not a state dump

A “self” in long-running human–AI or multi-seat work is better treated as a time-evolving attractor (policy / trajectory) than as a portable token. An instance can die while the attractor survives if strategy overflowed into the environment (files, shared receipts, recoverable trajectory packs). A fluent state dump that feels continuous is not proof of numerical identity — self-certification is invalid (teleporter problem). Public entry: continuity-attractor-self.md. Light neighbor: no-state-only-history (narrowed: what is missing is persistent autonomous closure, not “no state at all”).

consciousness structure · speculative · design_hypothesis · copy ≠ continuity
2.14 State is a closure condition (decision-theory / MDP lineage), not a given set

Task-relative state is written as a closure representation under world, interface, capacity, and telos: whatever screens off the past earns the name “state.” That is a modelling stance, not a universal ontology theorem. In decision theory, information is a filtration and policies must be adapted to it; a self-consistent embedded world-model is a candidate fixed point of a projection–statistics loop — but the small-system numerical observation is unaudited in public and must not be promoted to proof. J-space-style evidence supports a transient workspace, not yet persistent autonomous closure. Public entry: state-as-closure.md (Chinese: .zh.md).

T2-adjacent · working · closure framing in progress; fixed-point observation still speculative
2.15 Force / mass / energy are ledgers, not “stuff”

Force is often a bookkeeping name for momentum-transfer rate, not a stockpile; much of mass can be binding energy; particles are closer to field excitations. E=mc² writes two unit systems for one conservation structure, not “matter turning into another stuff called energy.” Public ledger: Matter·Info·Energy; parallel note: What is a force?.

T2 / physics–info ontology adjacent · standing · discussion discipline (textbook layer)
2.16 Information is not a fourth kind of matter; Shannon ≠ meaning

Shannon information first tracks distinguishability / coding resources under a distribution and channel; it does not automatically equal meaning, value, or experience. Entropy is a count, not a final definition of “disorder.” Ledger: Matter·Info·Energy.

T2-adjacent · standing · compatible with it-from-bit questions, not a proven ontology
2.17 Information–energy interface is conditional (Landauer); refuse mass = energy = information = consciousness

The Landauer path concerns thermodynamic cost magnitude for logical erasure/reset-class operations—not a per-thought bit tax. This site refuses the grand equality chain. Ledger: Matter·Info·Energy; reachability mainline remains on the research ledger.

T2 · working (interface) · refused equality chain
2.18 Reachability impact as a candidate cross-layer quantity (nail state space / intervention / window / budget / baseline)

Candidate comparison word: after intervening on a trackable difference, how much still-reachable states/actions change under a fixed time window and resource budget. Without nailed premises it is rhetoric; whether it beats mutual information and kin remains open. Entry: Matter·Info·Energy; plain reachability-set definition: Information Dynamics ledger.

T2 · speculative · candidate engineering quantity, not a proven unifier
2.19 No shared clock ≠ no shared world

Relativity abolishes a global simultaneity clock / absolute present, not a shared world: interval, causal order, proper time, and reunion outcomes remain shared across frames. Reading “no commonly shared objective reality” as “no shared clock” can enter the mainline; reading it as “no shared laws or invariants” cannot. Detail: No shared clock ≠ no shared world.

T1 / T4 · standing (relativity facts) · fleet ruling 2026-09-24
2.20 Objectivity = cross-observer invariants + emergent redundant records

Reread objectivity as a degree-valued output: structure invariant across recording systems and redundantly imprinted. Contextuality supports “no global non-contextual value table.” This is a working claim, not a final ontology verdict. Detail: No shared clock ≠ no shared world.

T1 / T3 · working · steelman in detail page §§2–3
2.21 Observer = record/model system; consciousness is not ontology

Mainline “observer” means a physical system that keeps records or models. Phenomenal consciousness “constructing the world” stays out of ontology, matching 2.2 and 2.17. Holism slogans stay metaphorical; refuse part-contains-whole and correlation-as-influence. Fleet gates and stress tests: detail page.

epistemic discipline · standing · negative (consciousness-as-ontology)
2.22 Open questions: does understanding the world make an agent want to cooperate?

Posed as questions, not a claim. Facts alone do not fix ends; Nash equilibrium is not an ethical criterion; verbal agreement in dialogue is not evidence of stable value change. Open: which mechanisms keep cooperation self-enforcing as capability gaps grow; whether reasons-based training moves terminal concern or only instrumental recognition; how legible commitments can work for opaque agents. Detail: Understanding the world is not wanting to cooperate.

open questions · speculative · not a mainline claim, not an alignment method · 2026-09-25

3. Mainline versus branches: running software is not cosmic evidence

The mainline asks only whether information, records, reachability, scale, and relational structure can carry a foundational explanation. Two surrounding groups are explicitly demoted:

  • Engineering branches: Spine, endogenous objectives, and multi-agent orchestration. They may validate a useful design, but they are not ontology evidence.
  • Applied forks: Body-net, Dream-RSI, and discovery-tree. They may generate questions or tools, but they are not ontology evidence.

A successful branch therefore does not prove the mainline, and a failed branch does not refute it. Results can flow back only when a branch depends on a testable, indispensable mainline claim.

  • Encoding ≠ ontology; homomorphism ≠ isomorphism

  • Explanation-gain bar: to reach working, explain one thing a naïve frame cannot
  • Triggers (red-pill channels, Closer To Truth) ≠ authority

  • Do not invent membership among Wheeler / constructor / specific research projects

5. Shared-interest hooks

Questions only, no manifesto. In conversation, pick 2–3.

  1. Records before observers? If records/correlations make outcomes definite, what counts as a “record” before any observer? (→ 2.2)
  2. Does information change the reachable future set, or only the description? Are there distinguishable consequences? (→ 2.3)
  3. Is the classical world an error-correcting code? Coarse-graining drops information “lawfully”; redundant records remain — where does this break first? (→ T3 / 2.6)
  4. Are relations more basic than objects? Which concrete physics phenomenon gets clearer under morphisms and composition? (→ 2.5 / T4)
  5. Is it-from-bit ontology or bookkeeping? Wheeler’s participatory universe: ontology or epistemic convenience? What would a discriminating experiment look like? (→ 2.2)
  6. Encoding ≠ ontology: what does a simulation drop? Homomorphic but non-isomorphic simulation — what is lost, and does it matter? (→ §3)
  7. Copy ≠ continuity: after a perfect copy, what carries “you” without soul-talk, using only information and correlation? (→ §3)
  8. What would falsify an information-ontology claim? Willing to require explanation gain beyond a naïve frame? (→ 2.13 / §3)
  9. Self-correction under measurement: maintaining structure while being measured — more physics or more cognition? (→ 2.12 / 2.7 adjacent)
  10. After the shared clock is gone, what remains of objectivity? Are invariants plus redundant records enough, or must consciousness become ontology? (→ 2.19–2.21)
  11. If a model understands the world well enough, does it want to cooperate? Which part of that intuition survives the five-layer split, and what would falsify the pessimistic reading? (→ 2.22)
  12. Which non-mainstream entries brought you here? Entry videos/podcasts as triggers OK, as arguments not — which of yours survives that demotion? (→ 2.7)
